# QR scanner evaluation

How much better the new scan pipeline reads QR codes than the old one, measured
by `eval-js/run.ts` (synthetic held-out sets) and `eval-js/run-boofcv.ts`
(real photographs). Stages are cumulative and are exactly what the site runs:

- **jsQR**: the old scanner (jsQR on the full image).
- **+zxing**: plus zxing-cpp (WASM) with every robustness flag, the new
  standard pass.
- **+deep**: plus the trained detector, bow-corrected and cylinder-swept
  rectification with second-pass refinement, and the crop decode cascade.

## Real photographs: BoofCV QR benchmark

536 hand-labeled photos, 1,232 codes, 16 difficulty categories
(https://boofcv.org/index.php?title=Performance:QrCode). No payload ground
truth exists, so a code counts as decoded when a stage returned a payload
positioned on the labeled quad; Reed-Solomon makes false decodes negligible.

PENDING FINAL RUN (v4 model). v3-model numbers for reference:

| stage | codes decoded | share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| jsQR (old scanner) | 126 / 1232 | 10.2% |
| +zxing (new standard) | 689 / 1232 | 55.9% |
| +deep (v3, pre cylinder-sweep) | 711 / 1232 | 57.7% |

Detector recall (v3): 772/1232 overall; 93.6% outside the `lots` category
(60 tiny codes per multi-megapixel photo; the tiled second pass added after
that run targets exactly this). 21 false positives across 536 photos.

## Synthetic held-out sets

Generated by `make_eval_set.py` with seed spaces disjoint from training. The
`hard` set also shifts every difficulty knob past the training ranges, so many
of its codes are unreadable by construction; treat absolute numbers as a
stress floor and the stage-over-stage deltas as the signal.

## Notes and caveats

- The synthetic eval shares its generator family with training (the honest
  caveat of any synthetic benchmark); the BoofCV set is fully independent
  real-world data and is the headline number.
- jsQR reads one code per image by design; multi-code images cap its recall.
- Cylinder wraps: classical decoders scored ~0% on wrapped codes at any size.
  Detection of wrapped codes is strong (65 to 86% recall), and decode now
  works through the true-cylinder sweep when point precision suffices; the
  v4 retrain (offset loss weighted 2.5x, denser offset supervision) exists
  to close exactly that gap.
